01 · POSITION

Position

UNITY should be sold as the field’s single signed data mesh, not as a sensor. One chip design sits inside every node: soil probe, climate station, valve controller and panel junction box. Each node signs its own readings, relays its neighbours’ traffic over Wirepas Mesh, and acts as a fixed ranging anchor for robots and drones. Confidence: high on the measurement and finance case, medium on positioning, which depends on silicon still to be qualified.

The value is not the readings. Semios, Hortau and greenhouse climate computers already sell readings. The value is three things no incumbent combines: one radio fabric for every device in the field, attestations a lender or utility can rely on, and a positioning grid that works under panels where GNSS degrades. AgriPV and greenhouse PV need all three, because the asset is financed on energy and justified on crop.

02 · PROBLEM

Problem

An agrivoltaic site today runs four or five separate data systems and none of them is trusted by the people who finance it. The inverter vendor reports energy. A soil-probe vendor reports moisture through its own gateway and cloud. The irrigation controller has a third app, the weather station a fourth. Every sensor in the popular “30 days of smart agriculture sensors” series ends the same way: sensor, gateway, vendor IoT platform, farmer insight. Each one is a silo.

That fragmentation causes three concrete failures:

  • Cost. Every vendor brings its own radio, gateway, SIM and subscription. Under steel racking and trellis wire, each needs its own coverage survey.
  • No shared evidence. Michael Barnard’s TFIE Evidence Book asks for separated, multi-year, measured numbers on crop value per hectare, measured water outcome, electricity per hectare and the agrivoltaic premium over ordinary PV. Vendor silos cannot join those into one time series, and nobody downstream can check them.
  • Self-reported telemetry. Voltiris is building its own VoltiCrop IoT and energy platform, per its CEAIF 2026 talk. At Eschbach Gemusebau the utility, Elektra Baselland, finances and owns the system. A utility underwriting an asset cannot rely on the module supplier’s own telemetry about its own modules. The agronomic half of the Voltiris return, about USD 20k per hectare a year, is the part no meter reads.

The gap is a neutral, signed, multi-sensor fabric owned by neither the module vendor nor the grower.

03 · ARCHITECTURE

Architecture

One node design, one mesh, one ledger. The sensor element changes per node; the radio, the signing core and the firmware do not.

Readings are signed on the node, hop across Wirepas Mesh to a gateway, and land in the Trust Engine. Robots and drones read position fixes directly off the fixed node grid.

Silicon. Wirepas runs only on Nordic, Silicon Labs and TI parts, and none of them holds a Common Criteria certificate. The near-term node is therefore the two-chip build already proven in UNITY v1: a Wirepas SoC (Silicon Labs EFR32MG24B class) for radio and mesh, plus an NXP SE05x secure element as the signing anchor at EAL6+. The fabless UNITY core is the route to one die.

Why no IP at the node. A Layer 2 mesh node exposes no network services. That is a documentable answer to CRA Annex I Part I point 2(j), which requires reduced attack surfaces, and to NIS2 supplier questionnaires. It also keeps node power low enough for a battery soil probe.

The industry has already published this design, wired. The EU REGACE pilot greenhouse at Fattoria Solidale del Circeo, Italy (Bovesecchi et al., Journal of Building Engineering, July 2026), runs the same split: 64 bifacial 75 Wp modules on internal single-axis trackers, PV-shaded sectors against unshaded reference sectors, and three sensor domains (PV performance, microclimate, irrigation). It does this over three separate Modbus RS-485 buses at 5-minute sampling, with daily CSV backup to a cloud folder. That is the right measurement design on a 180 m2 greenhouse. Cable runs, unsigned CSVs and no identity per reading are what stop it scaling to a 14 ha vineyard. UNITY is the same design with the wire replaced by mesh and the CSV replaced by signed records.

Precision fertiliser as a service: DOTS (Israel). DOTS, Data of The Soil (Rafi Levi, founder and CEO) sells continuous root-zone nitrate monitoring as a service. It reads nitrate in soil pore water by electro-optical spectral analysis, from seven years of Ben-Gurion University research. DOTS reports over 92% correlation with lab analysis and 30% fertiliser savings with no yield loss in tomato greenhouse trials (Farm Equipment, 24 May 2023). Its backers include OurCrowd, Labs/02, Reliance Industries and the Israel Innovation Authority. In emails to LGV, DOTS reported over 25% savings in two commercial POCs (20 Dec 2022) and was raising a USD 5M to 7M seed round to install its first 100 units (9 May 2023). An MNDA was exchanged in September 2022. A DOTS nitrate head on a UNITY node fills the gap in-soil pH cannot: a nutrient reading that drives fertigation, signed at source. Treat all savings figures as vendor claims until DOTS’s current status is checked.

05 · POSITIONING

Hyper-location and wayfinding

The fixed node grid can double as a ranging anchor grid, and overhead PV is where that matters most. Fraunhofer ISE measured GNSS under the overhead orchard array at Kressbronn: signal strength fell 3.7 dB-Hz and horizontal error rose from 3.8 m to 5.3 m. The authors recommend RTK or SLAM (pv magazine, 29 Nov 2024). A solar-site mower holding plus or minus 5 cm with RTK in open sky lost position under panels and fell back to odometry (Xsens case study).

Design call. The xG24 is both a Wirepas platform and the Silicon Labs Channel Sounding reference chip, so one-chip Wirepas plus ranging is plausible. Wirepas, or its reseller Siemens, has published no support for sharing radio time with Channel Sounding, so it is a vendor conversation, not an off-the-shelf feature. The low-risk build puts a second ranging radio on panel-powered junction-box nodes only. They have the energy budget and sit on a surveyed grid. Battery soil probes stay pure sensors.

Time-domain services. Two-way ranging needs no shared clock, which suits sleepy battery anchors. TDoA would need nanosecond anchor sync that a low-energy mesh cannot give. Millisecond timestamps for signed records are a separate and much easier service, and the mesh can provide them.

Who uses it. Vineyard autonomy is moving off GNSS-only guidance. Naio stopped selling and supporting its RTK-guided Jo and Orio robots on 15 June 2026 (Naio). FarmX sells a vision-based GPS-free orchard tractor kit. A standards-based anchor grid is the one positioning layer any robot or drone vendor that adopts Channel Sounding can use without a proprietary receiver.

07 · STEELMAN

Steelman

The strongest case: regulators and financiers now write measurement conditions into AgriPV approvals, and published results disagree so widely that only independent, signed, site-level evidence can settle them. A single mesh that also carries energy and positioning is the cheapest way to produce that evidence.

  1. Measurement is now a permit condition, not a nice-to-have. BC’s Agricultural Land Commission approved Double Barrel Phase 1 on condition that a grape quantity and quality report is filed before Phase 2 can proceed1. DIN SPEC 91434 makes documentation and operational monitoring of the agricultural main use part of the standard2. SolarPower Europe’s 18-country policy map finds France requires Agri-PV to keep at least 90% of agricultural yield and Germany 66%. France makes projects using non-proven technologies monitor yields against an adjacent control zone, and Italy’s “advanced agrivoltaic” status requires a monitoring system3. Barnard’s Evidence Book turns the same demand into five denominators4. Each one is a recurring, site-specific measurement obligation.
  2. The published numbers disagree, and that spread is the market. Voltiris reports +9% class-I peppers in the Delphy trial, but +1.5% at Meier Gemuse, its only hectare-scale commercial site5. Its strawberry turnover figure is +4% on its own site and +9% in its white paper6. Puglia vines under panels gave 277% more grapes in one season, while cool-climate Veneto trials showed yield and quality falls7. Sun’Agri reports 20 to 60% yield gains in the Southern Rhone8. A lender cannot price a range that wide. Signed, site-level, reference-zone data narrows it.
  3. One fabric costs less than five silos. Wirepas routes about 100 m per hop, takes up to 14 nodes per router, reaches 2,745 nodes in three hops, and runs non-router nodes at 12 to 15 microamps9. It already carries 702,046 meters across Himalayan valleys where cellular is thin, and over 12 million in India10. On an AgriPV site, the panel junction boxes provide a dense, powered router backbone at no extra infrastructure cost, provided each junction-box node carries a battery or supercap sized for overnight low-energy routing, because frost is a night event. The REGACE pilot needed three separate Modbus buses to cover the same three domains on 180 m211.
  4. Trust is built in, not bolted on. Every reading is signed under a hardware identity at the node. The node has no IP address, which is a documentable answer to CRA Annex I Part I point 2(j) on reduced attack surface12. Incumbent sensor platforms produce readings. UNITY produces attestations a third party can verify. The Double Barrel team’s own assessment names data interoperability as its primary technical risk, because no integrated precision agriculture data system exists23.
  5. The grid solves positioning exactly where GNSS fails. Fraunhofer ISE measured a 3.7 dB-Hz signal loss and 39% worse horizontal error under an overhead orchard array13. RTK-guided vineyard robots are leaving the market14. Bluetooth 6.0 Channel Sounding is a published standard that any robot or drone radio implementing it can range against, with shipping silicon from Silicon Labs (xG24) and Nordic (nRF54L15)15.
  6. The asset is financed on energy and justified on crop, and only one data system sees both. Voltiris prices about USD 20k per hectare a year of agronomic value next to USD 30k of energy, and no meter reads the first number16. Its utility-owned site puts that exposure on the utility17. BC Hydro’s Call for Power settles hourly with time-of-delivery factors of about 19% to 134%18, and new BC Hydro self-generators are paid 10 cents/kWh for surplus from 1 July 202619. Oliver is FortisBC territory, whose terms are not yet confirmed. Time-matched, signed energy data is worth more under both.
  7. Okanagan climate stress makes the need acute. The January 2024 freeze cut Okanagan insured wine-grape output from about 14,184 tons in 2023 to about 808 tons in 202420. The valley was at Drought Level 3 of 5 in September 202521. Dynamic Agri-PV over vines has cut water demand by 12% to 34% in European trials22. A winery that has lost two crops needs measured proof that the panels protect the vines.

09 · DOUBLE BARREL

Use case: Double Barrel Vineyards, Oliver BC

The pitch is not “add sensors to a vineyard”. It is “produce the report that unlocks Phase 2”. In January 2026 the ALC approved agrivoltaics over 2.67 ha of vines at Double Barrel Vineyards, part of Jesse Gill’s Okanagan Hills Winery Group. Phase 2, a further 9.83 ha, may proceed only after Phase 1 is complete and a report on grape quantity and quality is filed (Penticton Herald). That is 79% of the planned 12.5 ha waiting on one evidence document. The press calls it the first commercial-scale vineyard agrivoltaic system in North America, at a reported CAD 33M.

The site. Adjustable panels on mobile racks close automatically into a table-top cover, and thermal curtains drop along the row sides for sub-freezing nights. Irrigation is integrated, and there are attachments for weeding, pruning and picking (Penticton Western News). Gill lost his crop to winter two years running. Partners named in the press are Agrivoltaics Canada (installer), SFU, Western University and Claude Mindorff. On the 15 July 2026 call, Mindorff and Guy Price (Hunter West) agreed to use the project as the UNITY proof of concept, with Mindorff to draft an MOU. The project’s own platform overview, forwarded by Scott Lancaster, names AgriScout drones, the Mule ground robot and AgriCore analytics.

What the Double Barrel platform already claims. Randy McGuire’s Strategic Technology Assessment for Okanagan Hills Winery Group describes four layers: AgriScout AI drones, the Mule autonomous ground robot, AgriCore analytics, and smart solar. Drawing on the project’s own overview document, it claims the Mule replaces a 70 HP utility tractor for non-heavy tasks, saves 650 labour hours per 10 acres a year, and cuts chemical inputs 20% to 30% through precision targeting. It recommends a 10-winery Okanagan pilot, scaling to 50 and then 200 wineries, and calls for performance benchmarking protocols for yield, quality and sustainability.

UNITY slots under that stack, not beside it. McGuire names data interoperability as the primary technical risk, because no integrated system yet exists for precision agriculture data management, and answers it with AgriCore’s API. AgriCore is the analytics layer. UNITY is the signed field layer beneath it: one mesh that carries every sensor reading, gives the Mule and AgriScout their position under the closed canopy, and turns the 650-hour and 20% to 30% claims into logged, attributable numbers. Those benchmarking protocols are the ALC evidence pack, written once and reused at every winery in the rollout.

Why the closed canopy matters for positioning. When the panels close into a table-top cover and the curtains drop, the rows become a covered tunnel. That is the worst case for GNSS and the best case for a fixed anchor grid on the racking.

One line to confirm: the 15 July meeting notes place Double Barrel in Summerland at 2.5 MW, while the press and the Sunified 5-pager place it in rural Oliver.

Payback is a model output, not an input. Payback will be calculated, not quoted. It is set by configuration: panel density and tracking, curtain use, offtake route (FortisBC, PowerX, data centre), certificate value, the funding stack, and measured crop and input effects. Each configuration gets its own model run and simulation. The runs are workshopped with Okanagan Hills Winery Group, Hunter West and the EPC before any figure goes to a lender. Season-one UNITY data replaces the assumed crop, water and fertiliser effects in those runs with measured ones.

The report box is the product. Everything before it exists to make that report defensible to the ALC, and everything after it is the repeat sale.

Phase 1 instrumentation, proposed

  • UNITY in every panel junction box: the router backbone, energy record and ranging anchors.
  • Paired root-zone stations (moisture, EC, temperature at two or three depths) under panels and in open reference rows.
  • Climate nodes at bud and fruit-zone height under the canopy and in reference rows, including leaf temperature.
  • Valve, flow and fertigation nodes, with pH and EC read in solution. DOTS nitrate heads as an option.
  • Curtain and panel position logged as control events, so frost and heat protection can be attributed.
  • Harvest data tapped per row: weight, Brix, pH, titratable acidity.

Prerequisite: confirm that open reference rows of the same variety and age exist next to Phase 1. Without them the comparison falls back to prior seasons, which the 2024 freeze makes unusable.
